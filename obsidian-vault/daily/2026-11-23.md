- **发布时间**：2026-11-17

**中文摘要**

When processor implementation details are unavailable, black-box microarchitectural reverse engineering helps security researchers identify hardware conditions related to security vulnerabilities. However, this process requires substantial manual effort to design experiments, develop test programs, and analyze results for each processor. We present ProcReverser, a multi-agent system that automates this process. ProcReverser uses execution feedback to improve experimental plans and test programs and identifies microarchitectural properties from measurements. We develop a comprehensive suite of property identification tasks focused on hardware mechanisms relevant to Spectre-class attacks, covering speculative execution, branch prediction, memory dependence, and cache behavior. ### 2. CHERI under Fault Injection: An Emulation-Based Security Evaluation of Capability Hardware Enhanced RISC Instructions ⭐

- **类型**：论文
- **来源**：arXiv RISC-V
- **发布时间**：2026-11-15
- **标签**：`QEMU` `RISC-V`

**中文摘要**

Memory-safety vulnerabilities continue to dominate real-world exploit chains despite decades of research into software mitigations. CHERI is a hardware architecture that enforces spatial and temporal memory safety by augmenting pointers with hardware-checked metadata, thus preventing software-based exploitation, but its threat model explicitly excludes fault injection. This thesis investigates whether targeted fault injection can undermine CHERI's memory-safety guarantees in a pure-capability CHERI-RISC-V environment. Two fault classes are considered: bit flips corrupting capability metadata, and fault-induced check skipping on capability store instructions. We implement fault injection primitives in a QEMU-based emulation environment, which enables deterministic, repeatable experiments without specialised hardware. With them, we demonstrate four attack scenarios: buffer overflows, control-flow hijacking, data-only attacks, and use-after-free. Each succeeds with one or two targeted faults (lower bounds under the emulation-based setting). Check skipping offers an alternative route to control-flow hijacking and data-only attacks, with a minimum of one fault. These results show that CHERI's guarantees depend on the physical integrity of capability metadata, the uninterrupted execution of metadata checks, and the integrity of the revocation bitmap: a single targeted fault can circumvent spatial safety, temporal safety, or control-flow integrity. CHERI still raises the cost of exploitation, since fault injection is a prerequisite for every attack and the sentry mechanism forces an attacker to supply a valid tagged capability or spend a second fault restoring the tag. Based on these findings, we propose mitigations: ECC-protected capability memory, capability field authentication codes, redundant execution of security-critical checks, and revocation bitmap integrity verification.

### 4. Acceleration of Sparse and Irregular Workloads through Energy-Efficient Stream Extensions ⭐

- **类型**：论文
- **来源**：arXiv RISC-V
- **发布时间**：2026-11-20
- **标签**：`RISC-V`

**中文摘要**

The slowdown of Moore's law and Dennard scaling has motivated computer architects to uphold performance scaling through architectural specialization. So far, most efforts have focused on highly regular workloads, which exhibit good data locality, ample reuse, and a data-independent execution flow. As this specialization hits diminishing returns, attention shifts towards sparse and irregular workloads, which are essential to applications like sparse neural networks, multiphysics simulations, and graph analytics. However, they lack the convenient properties of highly regular compute that modern architectures are optimized for, resulting in very low (<10%) functional unit utilization on traditional CPUs and GPUs. The most promising approaches to their general hardware acceleration leverage memory streams, which decouple data movement from execution. In this thesis, we explore energy-efficient hardware stream extensions that significantly accelerate sparse and irregular workloads in general-purpose systems without specializing to specific application domains or compromising on regular-workload performance. We design a flexible, core-coupled stream extension supporting affine and indirect streams as well as intersection and union of sparse tensors. We integrate it into an energy-efficient RISC-V compute cluster, achieving significant performance and energy efficiency benefits on general sparse linear algebra, and further use it to accelerate stencil computations, spiking neural network inference, and model-predictive control. We evaluate it at scale in a 432-core 2.5D RISC-V system fabricated in 12nm FinFET, achieving leading compute utilization and energy efficiency on sparse and irregular workloads while remaining competitive on regular workloads. Finally, we extend chip-level interconnects with end-to-end irregular streams, improving performance, energy efficiency, and bus utilization.

### 5. Matching Architecture to Workload: Comparing RISC-V Parallel Accelerators for Energy-Efficient Embedded Computing ⭐

- **类型**：论文
- **来源**：arXiv RISC-V
- **发布时间**：2026-11-19
- **标签**：`RISC-V`

**中文摘要**

Embedded computing demands high performance and energy efficiency across workloads with widely different computational characteristics. Choosing the most suitable execution model therefore requires a quantitative understanding of how each model maps to different workload classes. In this paper, we compare three execution models for energy-efficient embedded computing: scalar multicore, stream ing multicore, and vector processing. We use three open-source RISC-V accelerators as representative implementations: the PULP Cluster, the Snitch Cluster, and Spatz, respectively. Their open-source hardware and software stacks enable a controlled comparison based on implementations in the same 12 nm technology and a common benchmark suite. Our results reveal that no single execution model performs best across all workload classes. Spatz delivers the greatest benefits for regular and highly vectorizable compute-intensive applications, achieving up to 4.29x higher performance and 5.17x greater energy efficiency than the other architectures. The Snitch Cluster performs particularly well on applications with limited parallelism and streamable memory-access patterns, providing up to 5.53x higher performance and 1.45x greater energy efficiency. Conversely, the PULP Cluster is most effective on irregular and control-dominated applications, achieving improvements of up to 5.66x in performance and 2.80x in energy efficiency. Compared with Spatz, however, the greater flexibility offered by PULP and Snitch comes at a non-negligible cost in terms of both area and power.

Continual learning (CL) enables neural networks to adapt to non-stationary data streams by mitigating catastrophic forgetting, offering a path to robust, private, and adaptable edge devices. ### 8. PULSAR-V: I/O-Aware Post-Update Attestation for RISC-V ⭐

- **类型**：论文
- **来源**：arXiv RISC-V
- **发布时间**：2026-11-18
- **标签**：`RISC-V`

**中文摘要**

Post-update attestation proves that a device installed the intended firmware, not that the image stayed protected until the report was signed. In a multi-master SoC, a master outside the attested processor can corrupt update-critical memory. In contrast, the firmware identity, lifecycle state, and the CPU's protection evidence remain byte-identical to a healthy device's. We present PULSAR-V, a post-update attestation architecture which binds the authenticated update commit to the effective permissions and immutability of both gates (PMP/EPMP on the CPU, the I/O policy mediating DMA), sampled at the commit and at attestation, the I/O gate latching its own commit-time state in hardware. In a RISC-V SoC prototyped on a ZCU102 FPGA (CV32E40S core with 16 PMP regions, IOPMP-style unit, AXI write guard), a 256-byte write from the board's Arm DMA engine bypasses PMP and corrupts the committed image: every conventional claim stays true, and PULSAR-V rejects the device on its I/O-policy evidence alone. It also rejects an intact device whose policy left the image writable, a verdict no integrity check can reach. The gate and its commit latch cost 436 LUTs, 4.6% of the SoC, and evidence collection takes 43.1k cycles independent of image size, 41x less than re-hashing 16 KiB.
