### 2. CHERI under Fault Injection: An Emulation-Based Security Evaluation of Capability Hardware Enhanced RISC Instructions ⭐

- **类型**：论文
- **来源**：arXiv RISC-V
- **发布时间**：2026-11-15
- **标签**：`QEMU` `RISC-V`

**中文摘要**

Memory-safety vulnerabilities continue to dominate real-world exploit chains despite decades of research into software mitigations. CHERI is a hardware architecture that enforces spatial and temporal memory safety by augmenting pointers with hardware-checked metadata, thus preventing software-based exploitation, but its threat model explicitly excludes fault injection. This thesis investigates whether targeted fault injection can undermine CHERI's memory-safety guarantees in a pure-capability CHERI-RISC-V environment. Two fault classes are considered: bit flips corrupting capability metadata, and fault-induced check skipping on capability store instructions. We implement fault injection primitives in a QEMU-based emulation environment, which enables deterministic, repeatable experiments without specialised hardware. With them, we demonstrate four attack scenarios: buffer overflows, control-flow hijacking, data-only attacks, and use-after-free. Each succeeds with one or two targeted faults (lower bounds under the emulation-based setting). Check skipping offers an alternative route to control-flow hijacking and data-only attacks, with a minimum of one fault. These results show that CHERI's guarantees depend on the physical integrity of capability metadata, the uninterrupted execution of metadata checks, and the integrity of the revocation bitmap: a single targeted fault can circumvent spatial safety, temporal safety, or control-flow integrity. CHERI still raises the cost of exploitation, since fault injection is a prerequisite for every attack and the sentry mechanism forces an attacker to supply a valid tagged capability or spend a second fault restoring the tag. Based on these findings, we propose mitigations: ECC-protected capability memory, capability field authentication codes, redundant execution of security-critical checks, and revocation bitmap integrity verification.

### 5. PULSAR-V: I/O-Aware Post-Update Attestation for RISC-V ⭐

- **类型**：论文
- **来源**：arXiv RISC-V
- **发布时间**：2026-11-18
- **标签**：`RISC-V`

**中文摘要**

Post-update attestation proves that a device installed the intended firmware, not that the image stayed protected until the report was signed. In a multi-master SoC, a master outside the attested processor can corrupt update-critical memory. In contrast, the firmware identity, lifecycle state, and the CPU's protection evidence remain byte-identical to a healthy device's. We present PULSAR-V, a post-update attestation architecture which binds the authenticated update commit to the effective permissions and immutability of both gates (PMP/EPMP on the CPU, the I/O policy mediating DMA), sampled at the commit and at attestation, the I/O gate latching its own commit-time state in hardware. It also rejects an intact device whose policy left the image writable, a verdict no integrity check can reach. The gate and its commit latch cost 436 LUTs, 4.6% of the SoC, and evidence collection takes 43.1k cycles independent of image size, 41x less than re-hashing 16 KiB.

- **来源**：arXiv RISC-V
- **发布时间**：2026-11-18
- **标签**：`RISC-V`

**中文摘要**

Hardware quantum error correction (QEC) decoders require validation of logical error suppression and timing compliance before deployment. Rare logical failures and deadline violations demand extensive sampling, while algorithm-level software evaluation does not directly measure hardware response times. Circuit-level noise models contain many error mechanisms but few firings per shot at low physical error rates, making exhaustive per-shot scanning inefficient. We present CHEQ, an FPGA platform integrating on-chip sampling, concurrent decoder evaluation, scoring, and RISC-V control. Its Sampling Units use geometric skipping to sample firing locations from detector error models and amortize mechanism traversal across shots within each batch. On an AMD VCU118 FPGA at code distance 7 and physical error rate 10^-3, two Sampling Units supply 20 Helios decoders at 49.2 million shots/s, completing 10^10 shots in 3.4 minutes without host-side shot generation.

Time-series sensing increasingly relies on neural networks, yet deployment on resource-constrained microcontrollers remains challenging. Aggressive model compression can reduce accuracy, while restricting weights to multiplication-free representations introduces further losses. SPEC CPU2026, the latest generation of the SPEC CPU benchmarks, is set to become the standard benchmark suite in computer architecture research, where new designs are often evaluated through sampled simulation with tools such as SimPoint.
